Agile Comrade Guide

Agile Comrade watches your active and upcoming Jira sprints, scores delivery risks from data already in Jira, and drafts AI mitigation plans, so you can act before a sprint slips.

1. Before you start

MVP software: a signal, not a verdict. The model can be wrong in both directions and sees only Jira. It can't know about someone on leave, a stakeholder changing their mind or a customer escalation. Confirm before you escalate.

AI privacy: what leaves your server. AI analysis runs only when you click Mitigate with AI or Scan with AI — never automatically.

  • Sent to the AI provider: ticket keys, summaries, statuses, story points, due dates and risk scores. Assignee names are replaced with per-call pseudonyms (dev-01, dev-02…) and emails are stripped from all text before anything leaves your server.
  • Never sent: real assignee names, email addresses, API keys or profile credentials.
  • Stays on your server: the pseudonymisation key (fresh for each call, then discarded) and your full Jira snapshot.
  • Encrypted at rest: profile and snapshot data. Deleting a profile in Settings erases its server-side data immediately.

3. The dashboard

Active sprints

One card per sprint. The gauge shows the score of the single worst risk, next to severity counts and progress. No risks shows 0 / ON_TRACK. Click Details to open the sprint. Each risk gets its own card:

  • Every card is headed by its severity and risk type (e.g. critical · 📍 Not Progressing).
  • Risk Signals: the measured facts and the score, shown as e.g. P4 × I5 = 20 → CRITICAL. How these are calculated is in the appendix.
  • Where the arithmetic shows — the chip strip spells it out (P4 × I5 = 20 → CRITICAL (80+)), and the collapsible Risk score matrix panel at the top of the page is the full grid, with every cell's band on hover.
  • Suspected cause and Suggested actions, with a confidence %. Draft Message writes a follow-up for you to copy into Jira; the app never posts there.
  • Human decision: mark it resolved, owned, accepted or mitigated, with a note. It's remembered across syncs. Add to Risk Register is a hand-off stub for tracking a risk outside the app.
  • Below the issue list, use Mitigation Plan with AI (current sprint) or Scan with AI (upcoming sprint). If the LLM is unreachable, an amber note shows a rule-based fallback instead.

Upcoming sprints

Run Scan with AI to catch unassigned work, missing points, thin acceptance criteria, oversized tickets and overloaded people. These use a count-based model, not probability × impact (see section 5).

Velocity trend

Completed points per sprint, per project, with an average, so you can spot dips early.

4. How scoring works

Probability × Impact = a score out of 25 → 0–100

Probability × Impact = score out of 25, stretched onto 0–100 so the number and the severity band always agree. A higher number means more risk, not more progress.

BadgeScoreMeaningResponse
CRITICAL80–100Sprint at serious riskAct today; escalate
HIGH60–79Likely slippagePlan mitigation this week
MEDIUM20–59Watch-list itemMonitor at stand-up
LOW0–19No action neededNone

Rows are probability, columns are impact. The big number is the product; the small one is the score.

Example: a 2-point ticket silent for 200 hours scores 5 × 3 = 15, which is CRITICAL 80, even though it's the smallest ticket on the board. That is deliberate.

5. Checks, confidence and thresholds

Active sprints run nine checks. Confidence is how sure the model is that the signal is real. It's fixed per check, so it reads the same on every run.

Upcoming-sprint checks (count-based)

Before a sprint starts there's no progress to measure. Each check starts from a base score and adds points per affected item, so severity rises with the count.

CheckFires whenScoreConfidence
👤 UnassignedCommitted items have no owner40, +10 per item85%
📐 UnestimatedItems have no story points35, +8 per item80%
📝 Undefined ScopeNo description, or thin acceptance criteria30, +8 per item75%
🏗️ OversizedItems of 8+ points50, +15 per item70%
🔥 OverloadedOne person holds 3+ items and ≥1.5× the team averageUses the matrix, from how far above average and how many points they hold75%

Overloaded is the only upcoming check that uses probability × impact. Both conditions must hold, so a small balanced team is never flagged.

Two numbers appear on an upcoming sprint card:

  • Gauge score: the worst finding plus 20% of each other finding, held inside the worst finding's band so it can't claim a severity nothing supports.
  • Readiness bar: items not flagged ÷ total items, coloured from the same score so the two never contradict.

6. What deliberately doesn't raise a risk

A tool that cries wolf gets ignored. These safeguards keep a healthy sprint green.

  • Silence is measured from the later of sprint start and last update, so tickets pulled from the backlog aren't punished for old timestamps.
  • Queued ticket, busy owner: a To Do ticket whose owner is busy is exempt, until fewer than 2 days remain.
  • Grace windows: burndown isn't judged until the sprint is 25% done, and Sprint Not Started waits 2 days.
  • A vendor mention isn't a blocker. Only a blocked_by link or explicit wording ("waiting for", "blocked by", "depends on", "pending from", "on hold") counts.
  • Bugs: only defects created in the sprint count. Only a fixed Highest-priority bug stays visible, at lower probability.
  • Due dates use your Jira timezone, not the server's clock.
  • Scope creep needs two readings. A sprint synced once is never flagged.

7. Data freshness

There's no background job. The last snapshot is cached for 5 minutes and reused. After that, or when you press Sync Now, Jira is fetched again and every risk is rescored. The top-bar timestamp shows when the data was pulled.

  1. Measure two things from Jira (listed in the table headers below).
  2. Look up each measure to get a probability (1–5) and an impact (1–5).
  3. Multiply them, then read the score and severity from the matrix in section 4.
