🎯 Agile Comrade — User Guide

Agile Comrade connects to your Jira Cloud, watches active and planned sprints, scores them for risk (0–100), explains exactly which tickets drive that score, and uses AI to suggest mitigations, draft follow-ups and write reports.

1. Configuring your Settings

Open Settings from the top bar. A profile holds one Jira connection and one AI provider. Fill in the form and press 💾 Save Profile.

2. Risk rules & scoring

Every risk scores 0–100 and maps to a severity:

SeverityScoreStatus label
LOW0–34MITIGATED
MEDIUM35–59MONITORING
HIGH60–79ACTIVE
CRITICAL80–100ACTIVE

Active-sprint rules

Each rule watches for one warning sign and turns it into a 0–100 score. The idea is always the same: start from how bad the situation is, then grow the score if time is running out.

RuleFires when…How it is scored
📉 Burndown Behind The sprint burns down >10% slower than the ideal line. The score starts as your % delay vs plan (e.g. 25% behind → score 25), but never starts above 60. It then climbs if the sprint is almost over or the delay keeps growing. So being 20% behind early on scores ~25, while being 20% behind in the final days can cross 70.
📍 Not Progressing An open ticket has had no updates for over 24h. One row per stuck ticket. Silence costs points: 1 point per every 2 hours without an update, up to 50 (a full day of silence ≈ 12 points, three days ≈ 36). Large tickets, tickets barely started, or owners juggling too many tickets add a bit extra on top.
🧪 QA Bottleneck 2+ stories are waiting in QA late in the sprint. Asks one question: can QA clear everything waiting before the sprint ends? Lots of QA work with plenty of days left → low score. The same queue when only a day or two remains → score shoots toward 70+.
⏰ Due Date Passed A ticket is past its due date without completing QA. Every day overdue adds 15 points, stopping once it reaches 70 (so 5+ days overdue all look alike). Very large tickets and tickets other work depends on score extra. Only the single most overdue ticket counts on the sprint card.
🔗 External Dep A ticket is blocked by another team/vendor via a "is blocked by" link. Waiting on someone outside your team starts high (75); waiting on a sibling team inside your own org starts moderate (50). Each additional blocker pushes the score further up, and large tickets score higher than small ones.
🐛 Bug Raised A defect was created during the current sprint. Tier comes from its Jira priority (Highest→P1, High→P2, Medium→P3, Low/Lowest→P4). The bug's priority decides which band its score lives in (table below). A fresh bug enters at the bottom of its band and drifts toward the top as it ages — e.g. an open P1 starts at 80 and creeps toward 90 the longer it sits unfixed.

In-sprint defect bands (🐛 Bug Raised)

ConditionScoreRationale
No in-sprint defectnoneNo quality risk introduced.
P3/P4 defect found10–20Minor impact; normal quality variation.
P2 defect found30–50Moderate quality risk requiring attention.
P1 fixed before sprint end60–70High risk, but contained before release.
P1 still open80–90Very high risk; work may not meet the Definition of Done.
P1 escaped to production100Maximum risk due to customer/business impact.
To flag a production escape, add the label production (or prod-escape) to the P1 bug in Jira — it scores 100 automatically, no configuration needed.

Next-sprint readiness rules (before planning)

These check the committed backlog before a sprint starts. Each problem found adds points, so a handful of issues is mild but a pattern is loud:

RuleFires when…How it is scored
👤 UnassignedCommitted issues have no assignee.Starts at 40; each extra unassigned issue adds 10 — nobody owning work is treated as an immediate risk.
📐 UnestimatedIssues carry no story points.Starts at 35; +8 per unestimated issue — you can't forecast what you haven't sized.
📝 Undefined ScopeIssues lack description/acceptance criteria.Starts at 30; +8 per vague issue — vague tickets become surprises mid-sprint.
🏗️ OversizedIssues are very large relative to the sprint average.Starts at 50; +15 per oversized issue — the most likely single cause of a failed sprint.

3. How Agile Comrade leverages AI

Rules detect what is risky; AI explains what to do about it. Your provider and API key come from Settings (§1).

FeatureWhereWhat it does
Mitigate with AI Sprint details page Analyzes every blocker in the sprint and writes an AI MITIGATION PLAN — concrete actions per risk category. If the LLM provider is unreachable, a rule-based plan is generated instead and an amber banner (⚠️ Rule-based fallback — may be less relevant than AI-generated plans.) appears above it.
⚡ AI Mitigation Strategy Expand any risk row A short, specific recommendation for that single risk. Always available — even if the AI provider is unreachable (rule-based fallback text).
💬 Draft Message Expand any ticket row Drafts a polite follow-up message for the ticket's assignee. Review it, then 📋 Copy or Approve & POST to publish it as a Jira comment (✅ Posted to Jira).
RUN AI SCAN Future sprint page Scans the planned backlog before the sprint starts and lists early risks per ticket. 🔍 View AI Prompt & Raw Response shows exactly what was asked and answered.
Graceful degradation: detection and scoring are pure rules and work without any AI key. Only drafts, mitigation plans and reports need the LLM — and those fall back to clear error messages if the provider fails.
🔒 Privacy: nothing that leaves for the AI provider contains real teammate names or email addresses — assignees travel as pseudonyms (dev-01, dev-02…) and are mapped back to real names locally before anything is displayed. Full details: Privacy Notice.
